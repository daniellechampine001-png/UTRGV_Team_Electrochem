Welcome!

We are Team 10, Electrochem, and this is our Senior Design I project. We are designing a portable device that detects PFAS in water right where the water is. The problem we are tackling is that testing for PFAS today is slow, expensive, and out of reach for most people. We hope you enjoy following along.

What is the problem we are trying to solve?

PFAS contamination is a growing concern, but the tools that detect it are built for specialized laboratories. People who want to know whether their water is safe face high costs, long waits, and few places to send samples. There is no easy way to check a water source on the spot.

High equipment costs

Advanced laboratory instruments are expensive to buy, run, and maintain, which makes routine PFAS testing expensive.

Time-consuming process

Collecting, transporting, preparing, and analyzing a sample all delay results.

Specialized laboratories

Testing needs trained personnel and sophisticated equipment. These labs are few and far between, so samples pile up into a backlog.

Limited on-site monitoring

Conventional lab methods are not suited to real-time monitoring at the water source, and most sources have no PFAS lab nearby.

Important to know

To understand the problem better, we researched PFAS and what it does. Here is what we learned.

What is PFAS?

  • PFAS is a classification of chemicals commonly found in manufactured products that are water resistant and non-stick.
  • PFOS and PFOA are the most researched PFAS chemicals and have an extensive history of environmental contamination.
  • They are primarily made of carbon-fluorine bonds, which are highly resistant to degradation because of their chemical structure.
  • Fluorine atoms tightly enclose the carbon chain because of fluorine's high electronegativity.

Harmful effects

PFAS shows up in everyday items, which is part of why it is so widespread.

Why is this problem important?

PFAS lingers in the environment and in our bodies, so knowing what is in your water matters. Today, finding out takes money and patience that most communities do not have.

$300+to test a single drinking water sample, according to the Connecticut Government
5 daysturn-around for FREDSense and Tap Score, about the fastest options available to the general public

Those five days mean five more days of drinking water that may contain PFAS without knowing it. And because few labs exist, the samples all funnel into the same places, which adds to the backlog.

Our proposed solution

We are designing a lightweight, compact, and user-friendly electrochemical device: insert a water sample, press start, and get your results.

Simple to use

No training necessary. With one small device and possibly some sample holders (not confirmed), a manual is enough for users to use it confidently.

Low cost

The compact size limits the material used, and with cost-effective material choices, our equipment cost is a fraction of typical PFAS detection costs.

Fast turn-around

The device goes with you to the site, so there is no transporting samples to and from a lab.

How the device works

Water from the sample is held and pumped to the sensor, which senses PFAS. The measurement is processed, a control loop feeds back into the sensor, and the result appears on the output display. A solar panel charges a supercapacitor that stores energy to power the system.

Sample water→Holds water→Pump→Senses PFAS→Measures PFAS→Output display
