Welcome to Team Electrochem!

We are Team 10, Electrochem, a senior design engineering team at the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley.

Our project focuses on developing a portable, compact electrochemical sensing device capable of detecting per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in water.

Our goal is to explore a faster, more accessible alternative to conventional laboratory-based PFAS testing.

What Problem Are We Trying to Solve?

PFAS contamination has become an increasing environmental concern because these chemicals are highly persistent and can accumulate in the environment.

Conventional PFAS detection often requires specialized laboratory equipment, trained personnel, and sample transportation. These requirements can make testing expensive and time-consuming.

High Equipment Costs

Traditional analytical instruments require significant investment and specialized maintenance.

Long Testing Times

Sample transportation, preparation, and laboratory analysis can delay results.

Limited Accessibility

Specialized testing facilities may not be readily available to all communities.

Understanding PFAS

PFAS stands for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances. These are a large family of synthetic chemicals known for their resistance to heat, water, and oil.

Their strong carbon-fluorine bonds contribute to their persistence in the environment.

Chemical Structure

Potential Health Effects

Exposure to certain PFAS has been associated with adverse health outcomes, including changes in cholesterol levels, immune responses, liver function, and increased risks of certain cancers.

Environmental Impact

PFAS can persist in water, soil, and ecosystems for extended periods. Their widespread use and resistance to degradation make environmental monitoring especially important.

PFAS in Everyday Products

PFAS have historically been used in products such as nonstick cookware, water-resistant clothing, and certain industrial materials.

Our Proposed Solution

Team Electrochem is developing a compact electrochemical sensing system to help detect PFAS in water more efficiently and closer to the point of sampling.

Our proposed approach uses electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) to study changes in electrical impedance at an electrode interface.

The sensing system will use a specialized electrode configuration, signal processing, and calibration to investigate PFAS concentrations in water.

Compact Design

Reduce the size of the sensing system for portable applications.

Electrochemical Detection

Use impedance measurements to investigate PFAS-related responses.

Accessible Monitoring

Explore a practical alternative to frequent off-site testing.

Prototype Early and Often

Device Concept

Electrode Stack Concept

Final Product Development

The project is currently in the design and prototyping stage.

The team is continuing to investigate electrode materials, sensor geometry, electrochemical instrumentation, and methods for integrating the components into a compact system.

Device performance and detection capabilities will require experimental validation.

Future Work

As development continues, our team will focus on the following objectives:

  • Finalize sensor material selection.
  • Obtain and assemble prototype components.
  • Integrate the electrodes with EIS equipment.
  • Evaluate electrochemical responses.
  • Investigate sensitivity and detection limits.
  • Improve the compactness of the design.
  • Validate performance using water samples.
